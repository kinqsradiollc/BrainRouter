# Evidence research

## Overview

Research begins with a falsifiable question and ends with an auditable chain
from claims to sources. Evidence quality, dates, scope, and conflicts matter as
much as the number of results.

## When to Use

Use for technical investigation, literature review, competitive analysis,
fact checking, policy research, or any answer that needs source attribution.

## Workflow

1. Restate the question, decision it informs, inclusion boundaries, and what
   would count as disconfirming evidence.
2. Search existing workspace sources first, then prefer primary and official
   external sources. Record publication and event dates when freshness matters.
3. Maintain a claim ledger: claim, supporting source, relevant excerpt or data,
   limitations, and confidence. Keep inference visibly separate from source fact.
4. Seek an independent source for consequential claims and actively look for
   credible disagreement, null results, or changed conditions.
5. Stop when the decision is supported, the evidence ceiling is reached, or a
   named gap requires new access. Do not pad the result with weak sources.
6. Deliver findings ordered by decision relevance, with citations adjacent to
   claims, uncertainty, contradictions, and the next useful probe.

## Verification

- [ ] The research question and boundaries are explicit.
- [ ] Consequential claims link to direct supporting sources.
- [ ] Facts, source interpretations, and analyst inferences are distinguished.
- [ ] Conflicting or missing evidence is reported rather than averaged away.

## Red Flags

- Citing search-result summaries instead of the supporting page.
- Treating source count as source quality.
- Omitting dates for claims likely to change.
